A Return to 1980

An alternative path to understanding the Cube

Learning Before Algorithms

To fully appreciate the Visual Structural Method™, learners are invited to mentally step back to 1980, a time before modern speedcubing, before widely adopted notation systems, and before the Cube became a competitive sport.

This perspective is important because the method did not originate from the speedcubing tradition. Instead, it emerged from observing and understanding the Cube's natural structure.

Seeing the Cube as a Structure

By setting aside the goal of solving as quickly as possible, learners can focus on something different: understanding how the Cube is built and how its pieces interact.

Like an engineer studying a structure or an architect examining a blueprint, learners begin by observing relationships, recognizing patterns, and guiding pieces through clear, three-dimensional movements.

A Different Way to Learn

In 1980, there were no established solving habits to follow, no algorithm sheets to memorize, and no expectations about the "correct" way to learn.

Approaching the Cube from this perspective gives beginners a similar opportunity to experience discovery through observation. Rather than focusing on formulas, they learn to follow the Cube's visible pathways, watching how pieces move, connect, separate, protect, and return.

The Visual Structural Method is not simply another set of solving procedures. It is a learning approach built on observation, structural recognition, and understanding. Instead of beginning with memorized solutions, learners develop an awareness of the Cube's geometry and learn to recognize recurring relationships that appear throughout the puzzle.

A Window into That Experience

This website provides a digital window into that learning experience through clear 3D animations, visual demonstrations, and step-by-step structural explanations.

The accompanying book serves as a guide to that journey, documenting the development of the method and making its structural concepts accessible to modern learners.
