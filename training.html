Training Cube

Visual Training Session

Movement Practice

When practicing the 1L, 2L, 1R, 2R, 3TL, and 3TR movements, always start with the green face at the front. You may rotate the cube at any time to observe how the left and right sides move during each action and to better understand the visual travel path.

To begin, press the Training button and set the cube speed to a level that feels comfortable. Then select any movement button to see the action demonstrated.

Focus on how the structure moves, travels, and returns. The goal is not simply to watch the turns, but to recognize the visual pattern and understand how the movement affects the cube's structure.

Visual Pattern Moves

Demo Sessions

S1, S2, and S3 are complete solving sessions. Every session begins with a unique scramble generated using Twizzle tools within the cubing.js ecosystem and progresses through all five steps of the Visual Structural Method.

Select S1, S2, or S3. Set playback to the highest speed and press Scramble. When the scramble finishes, adjust the playback speed and solve the cube by running Steps 1 through 5 in sequence.

Each step can be replayed as often as needed. Use the Reset button for a specific stage, or switch to Cube Control mode to examine the solve one movement at a time.

Understanding the Solve Sequence

The demo follows the same structural progression taught by the Visual Structural Method: Foundation → Walls → Windows.

Learners cannot jump directly between steps because every step depends on the structural state created by the previous one. Resets must therefore follow the reverse order of construction.

This preserves the integrity of the solve and allows learners to observe how the structure develops progressively.

About Step 5 in the Solved Examples

Step 5 normally completes the alignment of the final windows using the center pieces. In some solves, however, Step 4 may naturally complete one or more windows before Step 5 begins.

To keep the solved examples consistent, S1 and S2 intentionally leave the cube slightly unfinished at the end of Step 4 so that Step 5 can demonstrate the final window alignment. This allows learners to observe the complete Foundation → Walls → Windows progression in every demonstration.

About Step 1 in the Solved Examples

The solved examples on this website use a free-movement approach for Step 1. The result is exactly the same: the yellow corner frame is completed and the solve continues normally.

The book teaches a more structured Step 1 approach developed through years of testing and refinement. These solved examples demonstrate how the Visual Structural Method solves real scrambles from start to finish, while the book provides the complete learning system and the recommended learning path for new learners.
